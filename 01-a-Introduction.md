## Introduction

### What this Standard is

The Nova Institute Impact Accounting Standard (NIAS) sets out how the impact of an activity is to be planned, described, reviewed, accounted for and reported. It is published and maintained by the Nova Institute NPC, which is the standard-setter and the authority on the Standard's content.

The Standard is normative in two forms at once. The chapters that follow state its requirements in prose, for people. The same requirements are expressed as a machine-readable corpus — a glossary, indicator and methodology concept schemes, and data requirements as SHACL constraints — so that conformance can be checked mechanically rather than by inspection. Where the two forms could be read differently, the machine-readable corpus governs, because it is what validation actually applies.

### How the Standard is operated

A standard does not run itself. NIAS operates on the [Independent Impact](https://independentimpact.org/) platform, which registers projects and activities, hosts the review and reputation processes, records artefacts so that they can be audited, and enforces the Standard's data requirements at the point where data is submitted.

Independent Impact hosts many standards. NIAS is one of them, and nothing in this Standard assumes that it is the only one. Conversely, nothing in this Standard describes how Independent Impact must be built: the platform's operational choices are the platform operator's to make.

### The boundary between the Standard and the platform

Keeping these two roles distinct matters, because confusing them makes the Standard unusable elsewhere and the platform unaccountable here. The division is as follows.

**The Nova Institute, through this Standard, determines:** what an activity, an impact and an impact claim are, and what must be recorded about each; which indicators and methodologies are recognised; what questions guide activity owners and what questions reviewers must answer; the knowledge and skills a reviewer must hold to be eligible for a given review; and how activities, indicators, methodologies and instruments are scored.

**Independent Impact, as platform operator, determines:** the identities and accounts through which participants act; where data is stored and how it is secured; how artefacts are made tamper-evident and publicly resolvable; how reviews are commissioned, paid for and settled; and which standards it hosts, including any platform-wide default.

Two consequences follow. First, a claim of conformance to this Standard is a claim about content, not about infrastructure: the corpus is validatable without reference to any particular ledger, store or platform. Constraints that bind the Standard to a specific platform's mechanics are held separately, as a binding profile for that platform, and are not part of the Standard's core. Second, where this Standard describes platform behaviour — for example, that reviews are not required by default — it does so to explain the context in which the Standard is applied, not to impose requirements on the platform operator.

### Status

This Standard is under active development. The machine-readable corpus is the more advanced of the two forms, and the prose chapters are being brought into line with it. Chapters that remain incomplete are marked as such.
